# Skill: plan-week

**Use when:** it's the start of your week (or whenever you plan) and you need to
decide what to actually make — matched to your cadence and to what's been working.

**Reads:** `profile.md` (pillars, cadence, constraint), `memory/what-worked.md`,
`memory/published.md` (recent entries)

---

## What to do

1. Read `profile.md` for the publishing cadence and the user's real constraint
   (time / ideas / finishing). The plan must respect the constraint, not ignore it.
2. Read `memory/what-worked.md` — lean the week toward proven patterns.
3. Skim the last ~10 entries of `memory/published.md` so you don't repeat recent
   ground and so you can suggest follow-ups ("part 2", "the opposite take").
4. Ask the user for this week's raw ideas, or pull from a backlog they point to.
5. Produce a week plan:

```
## Week of <date> — plan

**Anchor piece:** <the one substantial thing> — pillar, angle, format.
  Why this one: <ties to what-worked or a timely reason>

**Supporting pieces:** <list, sized to cadence>
  - <format>: <angle>

**Repurpose targets:** <which piece feeds which platforms — see `repurpose`>

**The one that matters:** if you only finish one thing, finish <X>. Here's why: <…>
```

## Quality bar
- **Match the cadence, then cut 20%.** Solos overcommit. A plan that gets finished
  beats a plan that gets abandoned. If the constraint is "finishing," plan *less*.
- Bias toward an **anchor + repurpose** model: make one good thing, spin it into
  many, rather than making many things from scratch.
- Every piece must map to a pillar. If something doesn't fit a pillar, flag it —
  it might be a new pillar, or a distraction.
- Reference `what-worked.md` explicitly: "your last build-log video outperformed —
  the anchor this week is another one."

## Next step
End by offering to run `write-piece` on the anchor.
